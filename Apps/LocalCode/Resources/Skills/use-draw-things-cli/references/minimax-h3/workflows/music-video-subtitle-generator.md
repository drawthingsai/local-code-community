# Music video with lyric typography

Use for a music-led edit with intentional typography, identity continuity, and
shot timing. Use the shared [production workflow](../production.md) for assets and
assembly, and [H3 prompting](../prompting.md) for generation.

## Establish the song and visual language

Identify the actual master audio, requested excerpt, exact supplied lyrics, aspect
ratio, and desired visual treatment. Preserve the words and their order. Do not
invent sung lines to fill an instrumental passage or assume an instrumental request
needs subtitles. If exact lyrics are necessary but uncertain, obtain the text.

Choose recurring character, wardrobe, locations, palette, and camera treatment.
Keep identity cards, environment references, and typography designs separate in
the project record; they serve different purposes. Select the actual full-frame
asset used by each clip according to the available input interface.

Match pacing and typography to the requested genre and performance. Hard cuts and
sharp text entrances can suit dark pop or trap; a sustained vocal phrase may need
a longer shot and a quiet text hold. Build the rhythm around the actual recording.

## Plan on one master timeline

Mark significant beats, vocal entries, phrase endings, and transitions on the
song timeline. For each shot, record its global start/end, clip-local timing,
action, exact lyric event, and typography treatment. Make the intended words
readable at the delivery size, with deliberate entrances and exits. Keep important
faces and actions clear; text should contribute to composition rather than obscure it.

Longer songs need multiple H3 clips and assembly. Carry identity, screen direction,
palette, aspect ratio, and recurring props through the cuts. Use supported frame
inputs for continuity when available. Render enough usable action for the planned edit and
conform cuts to the actual music timeline.

Use H3 Ref2VA audio conditioning to guide generated rhythm or delivery. For exact
song reuse and lyric timing, place the master recording on the final editing
timeline. Mix useful generated effects under that track and remove duplicate
music or vocals.

If generated lyric text is unstable or exact timing is essential, use an available
editor to composite the exact typography, preserving the agreed visual design.

Review the complete export against the master: word accuracy, phrase timing,
legibility, cut rhythm, continuity, and audio at joins. Report separately any
requested lip-sync or text timing that the produced result does not achieve.
