# Papercraft stop-motion explainer

Use for a dimensional paper diorama that teaches through visible mechanisms and
staged motion. Use the shared [production workflow](../production.md) for assets
and assembly, and [H3 prompting](../prompting.md) for generation.

## Build an accurate teaching model

State what the viewer should understand, then map each important concept to a
character, prop, layer, label, or mechanism. Preserve causal and directional
relationships. A metaphor may simplify the appearance, but must not teach a false
process. Use supplied terminology and exact labels; obtain essential missing facts.

Design four spatial planes where appropriate: foreground framing pieces, the
middle action stage, background context, and a distant backdrop. Distinguish them
through scale, occlusion, shadows, and visible separation. A moving camera should
produce coherent parallax rather than slide every layer together.

Make the construction legible: thick cardboard edges, folded paper forms, tabs,
brads, joints, rails, pull tabs, and hinged parts. Give each mechanism a teaching
purpose. A pull tab can show a force moving a component; a hinge can reveal the
inside of an object. Avoid mechanisms that move attractively but contradict the
concept.

## Design characters, props, and motion together

If a character guides the explanation, establish a consistent paper-puppet design,
expressions, articulated parts, and scale. Match props and labels to the same
material system. Keep arrows and annotations attached to the relationships they
explain, with enough space to read them.

Use metaphor/palette studies and a storyboard when the requested production needs
them. For a single requested image or clip, prepare only the relevant assets.

Choreograph staged stop-motion actions with small increments, short pauses,
mechanical travel, and deliberate settling. Describe positive physical constraints:
the hand rotates on a visible brad; the card travels in its slot; the paper retains
its thickness. This is more actionable than appending a long list of unwanted
rendering styles. Preserve the handmade material instead of letting it become
plastic CG or a flat digital slide presentation.

Translate each shot into H3 syntax and use the actual clip duration. Add narration
when requested, with stable speaker identity, exact educational language, and
enough time for the action to remain understandable. Plan audio and joins for any
longer assembled explainer.

Review scientific/conceptual accuracy, layer depth, mechanism travel, material
consistency, annotation readability, narration alignment, and whether the viewer
can follow each cause and effect. Repair a confusing mechanism or staging choice
before adding more visual elements.
