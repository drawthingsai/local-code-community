# Brand promo video

Use for a brand, physical product, service, website, or application promotion.
Use the shared [production workflow](../production.md) for assets and assembly,
and [H3 prompting](../prompting.md) for generation.

## Establish the real material

Collect the supplied brand assets, product references, screenshots, verified claims,
audience, distribution format, desired duration, and exact copy. Keep a compact
source record tying each important claim or visual to its source. Preserve names,
logos, product shape, and real interface details. Missing specifications are not
creative blanks to fill with fabricated facts.

Choose copy language for the target audience and explicit brief; the language of
the conversation alone does not determine it. Keep the film focused on one message
and a clear ending or call to action.

## Choose a narrative that proves the message

For an app or AI product, show intent, a legible process, and a visible result that
demonstrates the value. Use actual interface material where fidelity matters. For
a physical product, progress from recognizable hero view to meaningful interaction
or detail and a use/result. For a service, establish context, the service process,
and a supported outcome. Do not replace the proof with decorative dashboards,
invented performance numbers, or unrelated futuristic effects.

Start with roughly five to eight beats for fifteen seconds,
or eight to twelve for thirty seconds. Adapt it to the available time and readable
copy; it is not a requirement to cram every beat into one generated clip. Give an
action setup, anticipation, execution, impact, and enough settling time to register.

Derive transitions from the brand or product: a real shape, a material, a color
field, a gesture, or an interface movement. Carry a consistent visual motif through
the film instead of adding a new effect at every cut. Make the end card usable at
the delivery size, with sufficient hold time for its exact copy.

## Execute and review

Prepare only necessary anchors, then map each clip to available CLI inputs. Render
longer films as a planned sequence and assemble them with available editing tools.
Use H3 native sound/music when appropriate; use the actual master track in the final
edit when exact supplied audio is required.

Check that the product remains recognizable, screenshots and text are legible,
actions demonstrate the stated benefit, and no unsupported claims appeared. Review
the complete edit for rhythm and sound continuity. If the deliverable is a concept
film, describe it accurately; an animated UI demonstration is not evidence that a
feature exists in a functioning application.
