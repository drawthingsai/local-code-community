# Paper collage explainer

Use for an editorial cut-paper visual explanation with tactile assembly and clear
conceptual relationships. Use the shared [production workflow](../production.md)
for assets and assembly, and [H3 prompting](../prompting.md) for generation.

## Translate meaning into visible pieces

Define the single idea each scene explains. Choose an action verb and a visual
metaphor that expresses the relationship: pieces separate, stack, connect, reveal,
exchange, or balance. Keep the explanation consistent with the supplied facts.
An attractive collage without a readable relationship does not explain the idea.

Compose roughly three to six meaningful paper groups per scene. Use photographic
cutouts or illustrated fragments with halftone texture, cream cut edges, slight
layer shadows, and a controlled palette. Make each group's role legible. Preserve
clean paper texture, cream highlights, and clear separation between groups.

Plan the completed composition first, then describe the assembly order. The still
should show a coherent concept at a glance; the animation should reveal how its
pieces relate. If only an image is requested, deliver that image without expanding
the task into a film.

## Animate the construction

Use discrete, physically readable actions: a piece pops into place, slides under
another layer, presses flat, makes a small settling bounce, and holds. Keep cut
edges and contact shadows consistent. Explain which piece moves, where it comes
from, what it contacts, and when it settles. Avoid replacing the whole mechanism
with a slow zoom over an already complete still.

For I2VA assembly, use the intended opening state as `--image`: empty paper or a
partly assembled collage. Describe the pieces entering, their intermediate
positions, and the final arrangement. A finished collage can instead be the opening
for a clip that animates its existing pieces. If an exact supplied ending frame is
essential, check for an input mode that supports final-frame conditioning.

For multiple scenes, carry palette, textures, scale, and transition logic across
the edit. Keep the teaching order explicit. By default, use
paper slides, taps, and rustles without narration, music, or subtitles. Preserve
that restrained treatment unless the user requests other audio or visible copy.
Write physical sounds in H3's soundscape and mark absent background music `N/A`.

Review conceptual clarity, readable layers, continuous piece identities, action
order, contact shadows, settling holds, and unwanted text. If the metaphor is
unclear, revise the arrangement or action rather than adding unrelated decoration.
