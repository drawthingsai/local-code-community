# Handdrawn live video

Use for playful hand-drawn animation that interacts with a live-action space.
Use the shared [production workflow](../production.md) for assets and assembly,
and [H3 prompting](../prompting.md) for generation.

## Anchor the effect in the physical scene

Choose a real surface, person, and physical action that brings the drawing to life.
Begin with clear contact: a finger traces a mark, a hand touches a doodle, or an
object makes a line. The first few seconds should establish that relationship,
not merely place a floating effect over unrelated footage.

Use flat, rough chalk- or pastel-like strokes with lively edges and a consistent
color identity. Let luminous marks retain visible grain, uneven pressure, and
handmade edges. Keep them flat against, or visibly emerging from, the real surface.

Follow one traceable entity through its transformations. Describe which existing
stroke stretches into a limb, bends into a new outline, or leaves a continuous
trail. Preserve recognizable color and line character. Each transformation should
grow from what is already on screen instead of replacing it with a newly spawned
unrelated object.

## Choreograph discovery and pursuit

A useful fifteen-second arc is contact at 0–3 seconds, first transformation
at 3–6, escape and chase at 6–10, an interactive surprise at 10–13, then a room-scale
payoff at 13–15. Adapt that arc to actual clip duration and the requested scope.
For a shorter clip, choose a complete contact/transformation/payoff action rather
than rushing all five phases.

Move through adjacent real space. A line can crawl from the desk onto a nearby
wall if its route is visible. Give the camera a reason to follow: slight handheld
lag, an imperfect pan, or a late reframing makes the discovery feel observed.
Keep the human involved through touch, pursuit, or reaction. Finish with a legible,
playful consequence rather than an unexplained teleport or a horror turn.

A first-frame image can establish the live-action scene for I2VA. When the request
is to add drawings to existing footage, use a supported video-editing workflow
that retains the actual source footage.

Write descriptive directions in H3's English sections for generation while
preserving the exact language of dialogue and visible text. Tie drawing sounds,
contacts, and human reactions to their actions. Review contact points, continuous
stroke identity, camera pursuit, physical scene continuity, and the ending payoff.
